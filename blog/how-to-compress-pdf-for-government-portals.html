📄 How to Compress PDF for Government Portal Uploads

October 2026 · 9 min read

Government portals cap uploads at 1, 2 or 5 MB, and a scanned document routinely exceeds that by a factor of five. The instinct is to compress the finished PDF, which works badly. The large gains are available earlier, at the scan, and knowing where the size comes from makes the difference between a legible 400 KB file and an illegible 900 KB one.

A scanned PDF is a folder of photographs

This is the fact that explains everything else. When you scan a document, each page becomes an image, and the PDF is a container holding those images. There is no text in it — the words are pixels.

So the size is governed by image rules, not document rules. A text-based PDF exported from a word processor holds characters and font references and is tiny. The same document scanned is twenty times larger, because it is now photographs of characters.

The four levers, in order of effect

LeverEffectCost
Scan DPILargest by farDetail, below ~150
Colour vs greyscaleRoughly two-thirdsLoses colour
JPEG qualityModerateArtefacts around text
Page countLinearOnly if pages are optional

DPI dominates because it is quadratic. Doubling DPI quadruples the pixels, so 400 DPI is four times the data of 200 DPI for the same page. A 400 DPI colour scan of one A4 page can be 3 MB; the same page at 200 DPI greyscale is often under 300 KB and perfectly legible.

200 DPI is the right default for documents. It is enough for a printed page to be read clearly and to survive being printed again at the other end. 300 DPI is worth it only for a document with fine detail — a signature, small print on a certificate.

Greyscale unless colour carries meaning

A colour scan stores three channels. A printed document is black ink on white paper, so two of those channels describe nothing.

Switch to greyscale and the file drops by roughly two-thirds with no visible loss. Keep colour only where it matters: a certificate with a coloured seal, a photograph, a document where a coloured stamp is part of the verification.

Avoid pure black-and-white (1-bit) for documents with a signature or a stamp. It produces the smallest file and turns grey tones into speckle, which can make a signature look broken.

Why compressing afterwards helps less

Compressing a finished PDF re-encodes the images inside it. You are compressing something that has already been compressed once, so you pay the artefact cost twice and gain less than you would have by scanning smaller in the first place.

It is still worth doing when rescanning is not possible — a document someone emailed you, or a scan from a shop. Just expect the result to look worse at the same size than a correctly made scan would.

What over-compression does to text

JPEG was designed for photographs — smooth gradients. Text is the opposite: hard black edges on white, everywhere. Pushed hard, JPEG produces grey halos around every letter and the thin parts of characters break up.

The symptoms, at 100% zoom:

  • Grey fringing around letters that should be clean black on white.
  • Letter strokes breaking into dots, particularly in small print.
  • Blocky squares in blank areas of the page.

If you can see these on your own screen, the official at the other end — who may print it — will see worse. A document that cannot be read is a rejected document regardless of its file size.

Merging does not compress

A frequent misunderstanding. Merging five 1 MB scans produces a roughly 5 MB file. Merging combines; it does not reduce.

If a portal wants one file under 2 MB and you have five scans, the sequence is: get each scan small enough that the total fits, then merge. Working the other way round means compressing a merged file hard enough to damage every page.

A reliable sequence

  1. Read the portal's limit and format before preparing anything.
  2. Scan at 200 DPI, greyscale, unless a specific document needs colour or more detail.
  3. Check one page's size. Multiply by the page count to predict the total before building the file.
  4. Compress individual pages if needed, targeting a per-page figure rather than guessing.
  5. Merge in the right order — name files with zero-padded numbers so sorting cannot reorder them.
  6. Verify the page count and the final size.
  7. Open it and read the smallest print on each page. This is the check that matters.

Common mistakes

  • Photographing instead of scanning, at an angle, so the page is a trapezium. Many phone scanner apps correct this; a plain camera photo does not.
  • Scanning at 600 DPI "for quality" and then compressing hard. Worse than 200 DPI with light compression, and much slower.
  • Hitting the limit by dropping a page. An incomplete document fails verification rather than upload.
  • Uploading a photograph of a screen showing the document. Illegible and obvious.
  • Leaving it to the closing day, when portals are slowest and a rejected upload has no recovery time.

Where these files go

The documents in question are mark sheets, caste and income certificates, Aadhaar and PAN copies, medical records. Most free PDF compression sites upload every file to a server and process it there.

Why Do Government Portals Have Size Limits?

Ever wondered why these portals can't just accept any file size? Simple answer — they're handling lakhs of applications simultaneously. UPSC alone gets 10+ lakh applications every year. If everyone uploads 20MB PDFs, their servers would crash faster than IRCTC on Tatkal day.

That's why most portals keep strict limits:

PortalPDF Size Limit
SSC (ssc.nic.in)100KB – 2MB
UPSC (upsconline.nic.in)2MB max
IBPS200KB – 500KB
DigiLocker5MB per document
State PSC portals1MB – 3MB
Railway (RRB)500KB – 2MB
Passport Seva1MB per page

IBPS is the strictest — 200KB to 500KB for most documents. That's insanely small when your scanner produces 3-4MB files by default.

What Makes a PDF File Large?

Before compressing, samajhna zaroori hai ki PDF bada kyun hota hai:

  • High DPI scanning — Scanning at 600 DPI when 150 DPI is enough for forms
  • Color mode — Color scans are 3-4x larger than grayscale
  • Embedded fonts — Each font adds 50-200KB
  • Uncompressed images — Raw image data inside the PDF
  • Multiple pages — Obviously, more pages = bigger file

A single A4 page scanned at 300 DPI in color = roughly 2-3MB. Same page at 150 DPI grayscale = 200-400KB. See the difference?

Which Compression Level Should You Use?

This is where most people get confused. Here's my honest recommendation:

  • Low compression (80-90% quality) — Use for documents with photos where clarity matters. Result: 30-40% size reduction.
  • Medium compression (60-70% quality) — Perfect for most government forms. Text stays readable, photos are slightly softer. Result: 50-60% reduction.
  • High compression (40-50% quality) — Use only for text-heavy documents without photos. Result: 70-80% reduction.

For sarkari forms, medium compression works 90% of the time. Maine personally 100+ documents compress kiye hain — never got a rejection for quality at medium level.

Understanding DPI — The Secret Most People Miss

DPI means "dots per inch." Think of it like this — higher DPI = more detail = bigger file.

Here's what you actually need:

  • 72 DPI — Screen viewing only (too low for forms)
  • 150 DPI — Perfect for government form uploads
  • 300 DPI — Print quality (overkill for online submission)
  • 600 DPI — Professional printing (way too much for forms)

If you're scanning documents specifically for online submission, set your scanner to 150 DPI grayscale. You'll get small files from the start and won't need to compress later.

Common Mistakes That Get Your Application Rejected

I've seen friends lose out on exam opportunities because of these silly mistakes:

  1. Over-compressing — Text becomes unreadable. If the examiner can't read your name, it's rejected.
  2. Wrong file format — Some portals want PDF only, not JPG. Double-check before uploading.
  3. Password-protected PDFs — Remove passwords before uploading. Portals can't open locked files.
  4. Corrupted files — Always open and check your compressed PDF before uploading.
  5. Wrong page orientation — Landscape documents uploaded as portrait. Rotate first!

Pro Tips From Someone Who's Done This 100+ Times

Yeh tips kisi guide mein nahi milenge:

  • Scan in grayscale unless the form specifically asks for color photos
  • Crop white borders — that extra white space adds unnecessary KB
  • Combine then compress — merge all pages first, then compress once. Better results than compressing individual pages.
  • Keep originals — always save the uncompressed version. You might need it later for verification.
  • Test before deadline — don't wait until the last hour. Portal servers are slowest on deadline day.
