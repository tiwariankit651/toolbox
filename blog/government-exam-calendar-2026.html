📅 Government Exam Calendar 2026

October 2026 · 9 min read

Missing a notification is one of the few preparation failures that cannot be recovered. You can make up a week of study; you cannot apply after the window closes. And the windows are short — often two to three weeks between a notification appearing and the last date to apply.

This guide is about the system for not missing them, rather than a list of dates that will be wrong by the time you read it.

Why a list of dates is the wrong thing to rely on

Recruitment calendars shift. A tentative annual calendar published in January routinely moves by weeks or months, exams get postponed, and notifications appear earlier or later than the calendar said. Any list of specific dates — including one on a blog — is a snapshot that starts going stale immediately.

What does not change is the structure: which bodies recruit for what, roughly when in the year they do it, and where they publish. Knowing the structure means you can check the right six websites in ten minutes rather than relying on someone else's list.

The irregular ones are where candidates lose opportunities, because there is no annual rhythm to anticipate. RBI, NABARD, SEBI and PSU recruitments appear when they appear.

The only reliable source is the official site

Aggregator sites and Telegram channels are faster but introduce errors, and a wrong last date is a lost application. Use them as an alert, then confirm on the commission's own website before acting.

Two things to check on the official notice that aggregators routinely get wrong: the last date including the fee payment window, which is sometimes a day or two after the form deadline, and the eligibility cut-off date for age and qualification, which is a specific date rather than "this year".

The four dates that matter per exam

For every recruitment you are tracking, these are the ones to record:

  1. Notification release. When the details become available. Worth knowing so you can read the syllabus early.
  2. Application window close. The hard deadline. Everything else can slip; this cannot.
  3. Fee payment close. Often separate from the form deadline, and an unpaid application is not an application.
  4. Exam date. Needed for planning, and the one most likely to move.

Record the application close date minus four days as your own deadline. Portals are slowest on the final day, payment gateways fail, and a document that needs resizing at 11pm is how applications are lost.

Eligibility is where applications are quietly wasted

Checking eligibility properly before applying saves the fee and, more importantly, the days of preparation spent on something you were never eligible for.

  • Age is calculated on a stated cut-off date, not on the exam date or today. Being within the limit on the day you apply means nothing if the cut-off was months earlier.
  • Relaxations are category-specific and vary by exam. The same category can have different relaxation in two recruitments.
  • Qualification must usually be complete by a stated date. "Appearing" candidates are accepted in some recruitments and not others.
  • Attempt limits apply in some exams and are counted in ways worth reading carefully — appearing for one paper can count as an attempt.

A tracking system that works

The failure mode of any tracking system is that it requires you to open it. A calendar that notifies you does not.

  1. List the recruitments you are actually eligible for. Not everything — a focused list of six is checked; a list of forty is not.
  2. Put each application deadline in your phone calendar, with a reminder four days before and another on the day.
  3. Check the official sites on a fixed day each week. Fifteen minutes, same day, so it becomes habit rather than a decision.
  4. Keep your documents ready — photograph, signature, certificates — prepared to the common specifications, so applying takes twenty minutes rather than an evening.
  5. Note the fee for each, because several applications in a month adds up and is worth planning.

The calendar entry is the part that matters. A spreadsheet of deadlines you have to remember to open is the same as no system at all — which is the single most common reason a candidate misses a window they knew about.

Preparing documents once

Across a season you will apply to several recruitments, and most want the same things to similar specifications. Preparing them once and keeping them named saves hours and avoids last-minute errors.

A folder containing: photograph at the two or three common sizes, signature at 10–20 KB, scanned certificates as PDFs under the usual size limits, and a text file with your registration numbers. Named so you can tell them apart — photo-200x230-35kb.jpg, not IMG_4471.jpg.
