Common Rejection Reasons (And How to Avoid Them)

I've compiled this from actual rejection emails and forum posts. These are the top 7 reasons photos get rejected:

  1. Wrong dimensions — Portal says 200x230 and you upload 200x200. Even 1 pixel off can cause rejection on strict portals.
  2. Face too small — Your face should cover 60-70% of the photo frame. Don't upload a full-body shot cropped to passport size.
  3. Background not white/light — Most exams require plain white or light-colored background. No curtains, no walls with patterns.
  4. Wearing sunglasses or cap — Obvious, but people still do it. Only religious headwear is allowed.
  5. Photo too old — Most forms require photo taken within last 3-6 months. If you've changed significantly, get a new one.
  6. Blurry or low quality — Over-compression makes photos pixelated. Keep quality above 60%.
  7. File size exceeds limit — 200KB photo when limit is 50KB. Always compress after resizing.

How to Take the Perfect Photo at Home

Studio jaane ki zaroorat nahi hai. Here's how to get a studio-quality photo with just your phone:

  • Background: Stand in front of a white wall. No posters, no switches visible.
  • Lighting: Face a window during daytime. Natural light is best. No harsh shadows on face.
  • Distance: Keep phone 3-4 feet away. Use timer or ask someone to click.
  • Expression: Neutral face, mouth closed, both ears visible. Look directly at camera.
  • Clothing: Wear a dark-colored shirt/top for contrast against white background.
  • Camera: Use rear camera (better quality than selfie camera). Keep phone at eye level.

This method works for 95% of government forms. Maine khud aise hi photo liya tha for SSC and it was accepted first try.

The Two-Step Process: Resize Then Compress

This is important — most students only resize OR compress. You need both:

Step 1: Resize — Change dimensions to exact pixels required (e.g., 200x230)
Step 2: Compress — Reduce file size to within the KB limit (e.g., under 50KB)

Why both? Because resizing changes dimensions but might not reduce file size enough. And compressing alone doesn't change dimensions.

Use Image Resizer first, then Image Compressor. In that order. Always.

Signature Photo Tips

Signature rejection is even more common than photo rejection. Here's how to get it right:

  • Sign on plain white paper with a black pen (not blue — it scans better)
  • Sign in the center of the paper, not at the edge
  • Take photo in good lighting — no shadows on the signature
  • Crop tightly around the signature — minimal white space
  • Don't use digital/typed signatures unless specifically allowed

Final Advice

Don't wait until form-filling day to prepare your photos. Get a good photo taken, resize it to the most common size (200x230), and keep multiple versions ready — 20KB, 50KB, 100KB, 200KB. When the form opens, you just pick the right file and upload. Zero stress.

Aur ek baat — always keep the original high-resolution photo. You can always make it smaller, but you can't make a small photo bigger without losing quality.
