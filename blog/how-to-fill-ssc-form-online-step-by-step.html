📝 How to Fill SSC Form Online — Step by Step Guide

October 2026 · 10 min read

An SSC application is rejected far more often for a filling error than for anything to do with eligibility, and the errors are a small, repeating set. Most of them cannot be corrected after submission, which is why the preparation before you open the form matters more than the form itself.

Do the eligibility check first, properly

This saves the fee and, more importantly, the weeks of preparation spent on something you were never eligible for.

  • Age is computed on a stated cut-off date, not the exam date and not today. Being within the limit on the day you apply means nothing if the cut-off was months earlier.
  • Relaxations are category-specific and vary by post. The same category can have different relaxation in two recruitments.
  • Qualification must be complete by a stated date. Whether "appearing" candidates are accepted differs between posts.
  • Physical standards apply to some posts and are stated in the notice, not inferred.

Read your own notification for these. A guide — including this one — can tell you what to check, not what your numbers are.

Prepare everything before opening the form

The form times out, and hunting for a certificate mid-application is how sessions are lost.

  1. Your signature image, prepared to the stated size band — commonly 10–20 KB JPEG, around 6 × 2 cm, in running handwriting on white paper.
  2. Good light where you are sitting, because SSC CGL now captures the photograph live through your webcam rather than accepting an upload. Other posts may still require an uploaded photo — check yours.
  3. Certificates to hand — matriculation for date of birth, qualification certificates, category certificate if claiming relaxation.
  4. Your exact name as printed on the matriculation certificate. This is the one the system treats as authoritative.
  5. A working email and mobile you will still have access to in a year.

The one-time registration, and the fields that cannot be changed

SSC uses a one-time registration that is reused for every subsequent application. Some fields in it are permanent, and an error there follows you across years.

The ones to check character by character before submitting:

  • Name, exactly as on the matriculation certificate — including spelling, order, and whether a middle name appears. Not as on Aadhaar if the two differ.
  • Father's and mother's name, same source.
  • Date of birth, as on matriculation. This is the document SSC treats as proof, so any other date is a mismatch.
  • Matriculation details — board, year, roll number.

A mismatch between your form and your matriculation certificate is the single most common cause of rejection at document verification, which happens after you have cleared the exam.

Category and relaxation

Two rules that catch people.

You cannot change category after submission, and claiming a relaxation you cannot document fails at verification.

A category certificate must be in the prescribed format, and for some categories must be within a stated validity period. An old certificate in the wrong format is treated as no certificate.

If you are eligible for a relaxation and have the documentation, claim it. If the documentation is not ready, obtain it before applying rather than claiming and hoping.

Choosing exam centres

Centre preferences are usually not changeable afterwards, and allocation is subject to availability.

Practical points: choose centres you can actually reach, in order of genuine preference rather than optimism; consider that the exam may be at an inconvenient time of day and travel the night before may be necessary; and remember that later stages may be at a different centre in a different city.

The signature, specifically

It is treated as an afterthought and causes a surprising share of rejections.

  • Running handwriting, not block capitals. Stated explicitly in SSC notices; a printed name can be rejected as not a signature.
  • It must match what you will sign in the hall. You will sign the attendance sheet and answer sheet, and a mismatch raised there is far more serious than an upload error.
  • Black or blue ink on plain white paper, scanned at about 200 DPI in greyscale.
  • Sign inside a drawn 6 × 2 cm rectangle and crop to it, so the proportion is right.

On compression: signatures suffer more than photographs because JPEG handles hard black edges badly. Keep it towards the upper end of the band — 14 to 18 KB in a 10–20 KB range — so the strokes stay solid.

Fee payment, which has its own deadline

The fee window sometimes closes a day or two after the form deadline, and sometimes on the same day. An unpaid application is not an application.

If a payment fails, check whether the amount was debited before retrying — duplicate payments are refundable but take time. Save the transaction reference and the confirmation screen.

Before you press submit

  1. Read the preview against your matriculation certificate, field by field. Not from memory.
  2. Check the date of birth digit by digit.
  3. Confirm category and any relaxation claimed matches documentation you hold.
  4. Check the signature image is the right one, not a photograph or an old file.
  5. Note the registration number and save the confirmation page as a PDF.

Download the submitted form and keep it with your documents. You will need it at the admit card stage and at verification.

Do not apply on the closing day

Portals slow down near the deadline, payment gateways fail under load, and a rejected upload with two hours remaining has no recovery path.

Treat your own deadline as four days before the stated one. The whole process takes about thirty minutes if your documents are prepared, and the only thing the last day adds is risk.
