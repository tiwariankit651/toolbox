📸 Government Job Photo Requirements — All Exams in One Place

October 2026 · 10 min read

Photo and signature specifications differ between recruiting bodies enough that a file prepared for one exam is routinely refused by another. The numbers change between cycles too, so the durable skill is knowing what to look for in a notification and how to hit it — not memorising a table.

One change worth knowing first. SSC CGL in the current cycle captures the photograph live through your webcam during the application rather than accepting an upload; only the signature is prepared and uploaded. Other SSC posts and other bodies still require an uploaded photograph. Always open the notice for the specific post before preparing anything.

The four numbers to find in your notification

  1. Photograph dimensions in pixels, or in cm with a DPI.
  2. Photograph size band in KB — both the floor and the ceiling.
  3. Signature size band in KB, which is almost always tighter.
  4. Format, which is nearly always JPEG.

Everything else in this guide follows from those four. For reference, IBPS's own instruction pages state a photograph of 200 × 230 pixels preferred at 20–50 KB, and a signature at 10–20 KB, both JPEG. SSC states its signature in the same 10–20 KB band at roughly 6 × 2 cm.

The band is enforced at both ends

This catches more applications than any other single thing. A 20–50 KB requirement rejects a 19 KB file exactly as firmly as a 51 KB one, and the portal usually does not say which end failed.

Aim for the middle — 30 to 40 KB in that band. Two reasons: you are clear of both edges, and some portals re-encode what you upload, which can shift the size by a few kilobytes. A file at 49 KB can emerge above the ceiling.

Ratio, which is where faces get distorted

200 × 230 pixels is a ratio of about 0.87. A printed 3.5 × 4.5 cm passport photo is 0.78. They are not the same shape, so scanning a printed photo and forcing it into 200 × 230 stretches the face horizontally.

Nothing at upload catches this. It shows on the admit card.

The correct order is crop to the ratio, then resize, then compress. Cropping after resizing wastes the resolution you preserved; compressing before resizing wastes the compression. If your tool offers "fit" or "fill", choose fill with the crop centred on the face — fit adds white bars, which are not acceptable.

What the photograph must show

Bodies differ on numbers and agree on this, because the photograph exists to identify you at the centre.

  • Recent — usually within three months.
  • Plain light background. White or near-white, no pattern, no doorway behind you.
  • Face forward, both eyes visible, neutral expression.
  • Even light, no shadow across the face. The most common quality failure and the easiest fix — face a window rather than standing with your back to one.
  • No cap or sunglasses. Prescription glasses are generally accepted if there is no glare hiding an eye.
  • Head and upper shoulders, with a small margin above the hair.

A plain bedsheet in daylight, photographed by someone else from about a metre away, meets all of this. An arm's-length selfie usually fails on angle and distance; a crop from a group photo fails on resolution.

The signature rules that cause rejections

  • Running handwriting, not block capitals. Several notices state this explicitly, and a printed name can be rejected as not a signature.
  • Black or blue ink on plain white paper. Pencil does not scan cleanly; ruled paper leaves lines in the image.
  • It must match what you will sign later. You will sign the attendance sheet and answer sheet, and a mismatch raised there is far more serious than an upload error.
  • Sign inside a box of the right proportion. Draw a light 6 × 2 cm rectangle, sign inside it, crop to the rectangle.

Scan at 200 DPI in greyscale. 600 DPI produces a file far too large for a 10–20 KB band, and compressing it that hard turns the strokes grey and broken.

Why signatures compress worse than faces

JPEG was built for photographs — smooth gradients of colour. A signature is the opposite: hard black strokes on flat white, with a sharp edge everywhere. Compressed hard it develops grey halos around every stroke, and thin strokes break into dots.

A face at 30 KB looks fine. A signature at 10 KB often does not. So push the photograph to the middle of its band freely, and keep the signature towards the upper end of its own — 14 to 18 KB in a 10–20 band.

Sources that work and sources that do not

  • Good: the original file from a phone or camera, usually 1–5 MB.
  • Poor: anything received over WhatsApp, which recompresses on send. You would be compressing an already-compressed file, and the blocking shows at 30 KB.
  • Unusable: a screenshot of a photograph, which has your screen's resolution; a crop from a group photo; or anything enlarged from something small.

If the only copy arrived on WhatsApp, ask for it again by email or a file link. The difference at these sizes is clearly visible.

Prepare once, name properly, reuse

Across a season you will apply to several recruitments wanting similar specifications, and you will need the same files again at the admit card and document verification stages.

Keep a folder with the prepared files named for what they are — photo-200x230-35kb.jpg, signature-16kb.jpg — plus a note of your registration numbers. A folder of IMG_20261015.jpg is how the wrong file gets uploaded under time pressure.
