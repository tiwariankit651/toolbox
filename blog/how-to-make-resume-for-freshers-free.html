📄 How to Make Resume for Freshers — Complete Free Guide

June 2026 · 7 min read

Bhai, let me be honest with you. Making your first resume is genuinely confusing. You open a blank document, stare at it for 20 minutes, and then think — "mere paas toh koi experience hi nahi hai, kya likhu?" I've been there. Every fresher has been there.

Here's the good news: recruiters KNOW you're a fresher. They're not expecting 5 years of experience from someone who just graduated. What they want to see is potential, skills, and the ability to present yourself clearly.

Why Freshers Struggle With Resumes

According to a 2025 survey by Naukri.com, 72% of fresher resumes get rejected in the first 6 seconds. Six seconds! That's less time than it takes to read this paragraph.

The main reasons? Poor formatting, irrelevant information, and — sabse bada problem — copying templates from the internet without understanding what recruiters actually look for.

Most freshers think "zyada likhunga toh impressive lagega." Wrong. A recruiter screening 200+ resumes daily doesn't have time for your 3-page life story. They want clarity, relevance, and proof that you can do the job.

What to Include When You Have Zero Experience

No work experience? Koi baat nahi. Here's what you CAN include:

1. Projects (Academic + Personal)

This is your goldmine. Every college project counts — that mini project in 6th semester, that hackathon you participated in, even that personal website you built for fun. List 2-3 projects with:

  • Project name and one-line description
  • Technologies/tools used
  • Your specific contribution (if team project)
  • Result or outcome (if measurable)

2. Internships (Even Short Ones)

Did a 2-week internship during summer? Include it. Even a 1-month virtual internship from Internshala counts. Mention what you did, not just where you went. "Assisted in social media marketing and increased page engagement by 25%" is better than just listing the company name.

3. Skills (Technical + Soft)

Be specific. Don't write "MS Office" — everyone knows MS Office. Instead write "Advanced Excel (VLOOKUP, Pivot Tables, Macros)" or "Python (Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib)." Rate yourself honestly — agar basic hai toh basic likho, jhooth mat bolo.

4. Certifications

Free certifications from Coursera, NPTEL, Google, or LinkedIn Learning carry real weight. A fresher with 3-4 relevant certifications stands out immediately. NPTEL certificates are especially valued in Indian companies because they know the exam is tough.

5. Extracurricular Activities

Were you the event coordinator for your college fest? Did you lead a team of 15 volunteers? That shows leadership. Were you part of the coding club? That shows initiative. Don't underestimate these.

The Perfect Fresher Resume Structure

Keep it to ONE page. Seriously, ek page se zyada mat karo. Here's the ideal order:

  1. Name + Contact Info — Phone, email, LinkedIn, city
  2. Professional Summary — 2-3 lines about who you are
  3. Education — Degree, college, year, CGPA (only if above 7.0)
  4. Skills — Technical skills relevant to the job
  5. Projects — 2-3 best projects with brief descriptions
  6. Internships/Experience — If any
  7. Certifications — Relevant ones only
  8. Extracurriculars — Leadership roles, achievements

ATS Tips — Beat the Robot Before Reaching the Human

Here's something most freshers don't know: 90% of large companies in India (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Deloitte) use ATS — Applicant Tracking Systems. Your resume goes through software BEFORE any human sees it.

If your resume isn't ATS-friendly, it gets rejected automatically. Matlab aapka resume kitna bhi achha ho, agar ATS ne reject kar diya toh koi dekhega hi nahi.

  • Use standard headings — "Education" not "My Academic Journey"
  • No tables or columns — ATS can't read complex layouts
  • No images or graphics — They confuse the parser
  • Use keywords from the job description — If they want "Java," write "Java," not "J2SE"
  • Save as PDF — Unless specifically asked for .docx
  • Simple fonts — Arial, Calibri, or Times New Roman

Common Mistakes Freshers Make

I've reviewed 500+ fresher resumes. Here are the mistakes I see again and again:

  • "Objective: To get a challenging position..." — Ye 2005 mein chalti thi. Replace with a professional summary.
  • Including 10th and 12th marks — Only if you scored above 85% or the job asks
  • Listing hobbies like "listening to music" — Unless relevant, skip it
  • Using colored/fancy templates — They look good but fail ATS completely
  • Spelling mistakes — "Reposible" instead of "Responsible" — instant rejection
  • Lying about skills — If you write "Expert in Python" and can't write a for loop in the interview, game over
  • Generic resume for every job — Customize for each role

Power Action Verbs for Freshers

Instead of "I did this" or "Was responsible for," use strong action verbs:

  • For projects: Developed, Built, Designed, Implemented, Created, Automated
  • For teamwork: Collaborated, Coordinated, Led, Organized, Facilitated
  • For learning: Researched, Analyzed, Studied, Completed, Achieved
  • For results: Improved, Increased, Reduced, Optimized, Delivered

Example: Instead of "Made a website for college fest" → "Developed a responsive event management website using React.js, handling 500+ registrations"

See the difference? Same work, but the second one sounds 10x more professional.

  • Adequate white space — don't cram everything
  • No photos (most Indian companies don't need it)

Final Tips — Jo Main Khud Follow Karta Hoon

Tailor every resume. Sending 10 customized resumes gets more interviews than sending 100 generic ones. I've seen this pattern consistently.

Get it reviewed. Ask a senior, a placement cell coordinator, or use our ATS checker. Fresh eyes catch mistakes you'll never see yourself.

Keep updating. Finished a new certification? Add it. Completed a project? Update immediately. Don't wait until you need to apply somewhere.

Remember — your resume is not your life story. It's a marketing document. Its only job is to get you an interview. Ab jao aur ek killer resume banao. You've got this! 💪
