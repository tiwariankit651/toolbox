🌐 How to Check Your IP Address Online for Free

May 2026 · 4 min read

Your IP address is your digital fingerprint on the internet. Every time you visit a website, send an email, or stream a video, your IP address is exposed to the services you connect to. Understanding what your IP reveals — and how to check it — is a fundamental step toward better online privacy. In this guide, we explain why you should check your IP address, how to verify your VPN is working, and practical tips to protect your identity online.

What Is an IP Address?

An IP (Internet Protocol) address is a unique numerical label assigned to every device connected to the internet. It serves two primary purposes: identifying your device on a network and providing your approximate geographic location. There are two types — IPv4 (e.g., 192.168.1.1) and IPv6 (a longer format designed to accommodate more devices). Your ISP assigns you a public IP address that websites can see when you connect to them.

Why You Should Check Your IP Address

  • VPN verification — confirm your VPN is actually masking your real IP before accessing sensitive content
  • Privacy awareness — understand what information you are exposing to every website you visit
  • Troubleshooting — diagnose network issues, check if your IP has changed, or verify DNS settings
  • Security checks — detect if someone is using your network without authorization
  • Geo-restriction testing — verify which country your IP appears to be from

How to Verify Your VPN Is Working

Many people pay for VPN services but never confirm they are actually working. A VPN leak can expose your real IP address even when you think you are protected. Here is how to verify:

  1. Open the FreeToolHubs IP Lookup Tool without your VPN connected and note your real IP
  2. Connect to your VPN and choose a server in a different country
  3. Refresh the IP lookup tool — your IP should now show a different address and location
  4. If your real IP still appears, your VPN has a leak and you should contact your provider

Common VPN Leak Types

  • DNS leaks — your DNS requests bypass the VPN tunnel, revealing browsing activity to your ISP
  • WebRTC leaks — browser WebRTC features can expose your real IP even through a VPN
  • IPv6 leaks — some VPNs only tunnel IPv4 traffic, leaving IPv6 connections unprotected

What Your IP Address Reveals About You

When someone has your IP address, they can typically determine:

  • Your approximate city and region (not your exact street address)
  • Your Internet Service Provider (ISP)
  • Your connection type (residential, business, mobile)
  • Your country and time zone

While an IP alone cannot identify you personally, combined with other data points it can be used to build a profile of your online activity.

Essential Privacy Tips

Use a Reputable VPN

A VPN encrypts your traffic and masks your real IP. Choose a provider with a strict no-logs policy, kill switch feature, and servers in multiple countries. Avoid free VPNs that may sell your data.

Check Your IP Regularly

Make it a habit to verify your IP address before accessing sensitive accounts or performing financial transactions. Use our IP lookup tool for instant results without any signup.

Disable WebRTC in Your Browser

WebRTC can leak your real IP even when using a VPN. In Firefox, go to about:config and set media.peerconnection.enabled to false. For Chrome, use a WebRTC-blocking extension.

Use HTTPS Everywhere

Always ensure websites use HTTPS. While this does not hide your IP, it encrypts the data you send and receive, preventing eavesdropping on your activity.

Consider a Privacy-Focused DNS

Switch from your ISP's default DNS to privacy-focused alternatives like Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Quad9 (9.9.9.9). These services do not log your queries and often provide faster resolution times.

When to Be Concerned About Your IP

Your IP address being visible is normal internet behavior — it is required for communication. However, you should take extra precautions when using public Wi-Fi networks, accessing banking or medical portals, downloading sensitive files, or communicating about confidential matters. In these situations, always verify your VPN connection is active and your real IP is hidden.
