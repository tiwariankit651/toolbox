💰 CTC to In-Hand Salary — How to Calculate Your Take-Home Pay

May 2026 · 5 min read

You just received a job offer with a CTC of ₹10 lakhs per annum. Exciting! But how much will you actually receive in your bank account every month? The difference between CTC and in-hand salary confuses many professionals, especially freshers. This guide breaks down every component so you know exactly what to expect.

What is CTC?

CTC stands for Cost to Company. It is the total amount a company spends on an employee in a year. This includes your salary, benefits, employer contributions, and sometimes even the cost of training or equipment. CTC is NOT the amount you take home — it is always higher than your in-hand salary.

Components of CTC

A typical CTC package includes these components:

1. Basic Salary (40–50% of CTC)

The fixed core component of your salary. Most other components are calculated as a percentage of basic salary. A higher basic means higher PF contributions and higher taxable income.

2. House Rent Allowance (HRA)

Usually 40–50% of basic salary. HRA is partially or fully tax-exempt if you live in rented accommodation. Metro city employees get 50% of basic; non-metro employees get 40%.

3. Dearness Allowance (DA)

Common in government and older private sector companies. It compensates for inflation and is fully taxable.

4. Special Allowance

A flexible component that makes up the remaining salary amount. Fully taxable with no exemptions.

5. Employer's PF Contribution (12% of Basic)

Your employer contributes 12% of your basic salary to your Provident Fund account. This is part of CTC but you do not receive it monthly — it goes to your PF account.

6. Gratuity

Calculated as (Basic × 15/26 × years of service). Companies include an annual gratuity provision in CTC even though you only receive it after 5 years of service.

7. Insurance and Other Benefits

Health insurance premiums, life insurance, meal coupons, and other perks are often included in CTC.

Deductions from Gross Salary

Before your salary reaches your bank account, these amounts are deducted:

  • Employee PF (12% of Basic) — your mandatory contribution to Provident Fund
  • Professional Tax — ₹200/month in most states (varies by state)
  • Income Tax (TDS) — deducted monthly based on your tax slab and declared investments

Formula: CTC to In-Hand Salary

Here is the simplified calculation:

  • Gross Salary = CTC − Employer PF − Gratuity − Insurance
  • Net Salary (In-Hand) = Gross Salary − Employee PF − Professional Tax − Income Tax

Example: ₹10 LPA CTC Breakdown

  • Basic Salary: ₹4,16,667/year (₹34,722/month)
  • HRA: ₹2,08,333/year
  • Special Allowance: ₹1,75,000/year
  • Employer PF: ₹50,000/year
  • Gratuity: ₹24,038/year
  • Insurance: ₹25,962/year

Monthly deductions: Employee PF (₹4,167) + Professional Tax (₹200) + TDS (~₹5,000) = ~₹9,367

Approximate in-hand salary: ₹57,300/month (from ₹10 LPA CTC)

Tips to Maximize Take-Home Pay

  • Declare rent receipts to claim HRA exemption and reduce TDS
  • Invest in 80C instruments (PPF, ELSS, LIC) to save up to ₹1.5 lakh in tax
  • Claim NPS deduction under 80CCD(1B) for additional ₹50,000 benefit
  • Submit medical insurance premium receipts under 80D
  • Choose the new tax regime if you have fewer deductions to claim
