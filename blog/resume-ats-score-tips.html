📄 Resume ATS Score Tips — How to Beat Applicant Tracking Systems

May 2026 · 5 min read

You have spent hours crafting the perfect resume, but you never hear back from employers. The problem might not be your qualifications — it could be that your resume never reaches a human. Over 90% of large companies use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to filter resumes before a recruiter ever sees them. This guide explains what ATS is, how it works, and how to optimize your resume to pass through it.

What Is an ATS?

An Applicant Tracking System is software that companies use to manage job applications. It scans, parses, and ranks resumes based on keywords, formatting, and relevance to the job description. Resumes that score below a threshold are automatically rejected — regardless of the candidate's actual qualifications.

  • Parsing — the ATS extracts text from your resume and categorizes it into fields (name, experience, education, skills)
  • Keyword matching — it compares your resume content against the job description
  • Ranking — candidates are scored and ranked; only top scores get reviewed by humans

Why Most Resumes Fail ATS

  • Wrong file format — images, infographics, and complex PDFs cannot be parsed
  • Missing keywords — your resume does not contain the terms the job posting uses
  • Fancy formatting — tables, columns, headers/footers, and text boxes confuse parsers
  • Non-standard section headings — "My Journey" instead of "Work Experience"
  • Graphics and icons — ATS cannot read images, including skill-level bars

How to Optimize Your Resume for ATS

1. Use the Right Keywords

Read the job description carefully and identify key skills, tools, and qualifications mentioned. Include these exact terms in your resume. If the job says "project management," do not write "managing projects" — use the exact phrase.

2. Use Standard Section Headings

  • Work Experience (not "Career Journey")
  • Education (not "Academic Background")
  • Skills (not "What I Bring")
  • Certifications (not "Credentials")

3. Keep Formatting Simple

  • Use a single-column layout
  • Stick to standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman)
  • Avoid tables, text boxes, and columns
  • Use bullet points (standard dots, not custom symbols)
  • Submit as .docx or simple PDF

4. Include a Skills Section

Create a dedicated skills section listing relevant technical and soft skills using the exact terminology from the job posting. This gives the ATS a concentrated block of keywords to match.

5. Spell Out Acronyms

Write both the full term and abbreviation: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)" — this ensures a match regardless of which version the ATS searches for.

Keyword Optimization Strategy

  1. Copy the job description into a text file
  2. Highlight repeated skills, tools, and qualifications
  3. Check your resume for each highlighted term
  4. Add missing terms naturally into your experience descriptions
  5. Use the FreeToolHubs ATS Checker to score your resume against the job description

Common ATS Mistakes to Avoid

  • One resume for all jobs — tailor your resume for each application
  • Keyword stuffing — repeating terms unnaturally will flag your resume as spam
  • Using images for contact info — ATS cannot read text inside images
  • Putting info in headers/footers — many ATS systems skip these areas
  • Creative file names — use "FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf"
