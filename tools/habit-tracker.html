The 21-day figure is not from research

It originates in a 1960 observation by a plastic surgeon that patients took about three weeks to adjust to a changed appearance. It was never a study of habit formation and has been repeated ever since.

The actual research is less tidy. A study tracking people forming everyday habits found a median of around 66 days to reach automaticity, with a very wide spread — some simple habits in under three weeks, some complex ones past eight months.

The range matters more than the median: a habit that has not stuck in three weeks is not a failure, it is normal. The signal to watch for is not a date but the day you do the thing without having decided to.

The cue is the part people leave out

A habit is a cue, a response and a reward. Most attempts specify only the response — "study more" — which means every occurrence requires a fresh decision.

A cue has to be something that reliably happens and that you do not control. "After I brush my teeth" works because you brush your teeth. "When I feel motivated" is not a cue at all.

The strongest form is an existing habit: after [thing I already do], I will [new thing]. Note that each of these names a specific observable moment — "in the morning" is not a moment.

Start smaller than feels worthwhile

This is counter-intuitive and decides success. "Two hours daily" fails because on a bad day it is impossible, and a missed day breaks the chain. "Open the book and read one page" survives bad days, and the repetition is the habit — not the volume.

Once the behaviour is automatic, growing it is easy. Getting a two-hour commitment to become automatic is not. A useful sizing test: could you do it on your worst day, tired and busy? If not, it is too big yet.

Friction decides more than willpower

The number of steps between cue and action predicts whether it happens. To build a habit, remove steps — book on the pillow, cards open in a tab, clothes laid out. To break one, add steps — log out, phone in another room, delete the shortcut.

None of these make anything impossible. They make it require a decision, and a decision is enough to interrupt an automatic behaviour. This is why environment beats intention.

What tracking is actually for

Two things. It gives immediate feedback for a behaviour whose real reward is distant — studying today produces no visible result, marking the day does. And it shows the pattern: a streak with Sundays always missed is information, because Sunday's cue does not exist.

One missed day has almost no effect on habit formation. The damage comes from the second consecutive miss, where the pattern re-forms around not doing it. So the rule worth following is never miss twice — and a broken streak is not a reason to abandon the habit, because the streak was the motivator and not the goal.

One at a time

Each new habit needs attention in the early weeks, and attention is the limited resource. One habit established in two months, then the next, produces six a year. Five attempted at once produces none.

About This Tool

Build better daily habits with streak tracking and weekly reports. Add your habits (study 2 hours, exercise, read newspaper, drink water, etc.), check them off each day, and watch your streaks grow. The 7-day visual history shows your consistency at a glance. Weekly bar chart report shows completion percentage for each day. See total habits, completed today, and overall completion rate. Frequently Asked Questions

Is this tool really free?

Yes. Habit Tracker has no charge, no sign-up and no paid tier. The .csv/.json output carries no watermark and there is no cap on how often you use it. Habit Tracker keeps its state in your own browser storage, so there is no account to fund and the site runs on advertising instead.

Is my data safe?

Yes, and the reason is in the architecture rather than in a policy. Habit Tracker reads your JSON file with the browser's own file API and does the work on your device — there is no upload step at all, encrypted or otherwise. Anything remembered between visits sits in local storage under habits, which is on your device and invisible to us. Clearing browser data clears it.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes. Habit Tracker runs in the browser rather than as an installed application, so a phone, a tablet and a desktop behave the same.

Does it work offline?

Yes, after the first visit. Habit Tracker is cached by a service worker, so opening it again works with no connection. What you saved is under habits in this browser, which is readable with no connection. Handling a JSON file was never a networked operation in Habit Tracker, so offline is not a reduced mode.

Can I use this for commercial purposes?

Yes. Whatever Habit Tracker produces from your JSON file is yours for any purpose, personal or commercial, with no attribution required. The output derives entirely from the JSON file you supplied. The .csv file Habit Tracker produces is written on your machine and never passes through ours, so there is nothing for us to hold a claim over.
