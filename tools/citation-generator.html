📖 Citation Style Quick Reference

Not sure which style to use? Here is when each is typically required and the mistakes to avoid.

APA 7th — Psychology, education, social & health sciences

Author-date system. Reference list sorted alphabetically. In-text: (Author, Year). Common mistakes: forgetting the year, not using a hanging indent, and italicising the wrong element (book/journal titles are italic, article titles are not).

MLA 9th — Humanities, literature, languages

Author-page system. Works Cited list. In-text: (Author 42). Use "et al." for 3+ authors. Common mistakes: adding the year in the in-text citation, and omitting the medium/container.

Chicago — History, arts (two variants)

Notes-Bibliography uses footnotes/endnotes plus a bibliography (common in humanities). Author-Date uses (Author Year) parenthetical citations (common in sciences). Common mistake: mixing the two systems in one paper.

Harvard — Widely used in UK/Australia, business & sciences

Author-date, similar to APA but with variations by institution. In-text: (Author Year). Common mistake: assuming one universal Harvard format — always check your institution's guide.

IEEE — Engineering, computer science, electronics

Numbered system. References listed in the order cited, in-text as [1], [2]. Common mistake: sorting references alphabetically instead of by citation order.

Vancouver — Medicine & biomedical sciences

Numbered system like IEEE. In-text as superscript or (1). Journal names are abbreviated. Common mistake: not abbreviating journal titles and mis-ordering references.
