How to Use This Tool

  1. Set the reference time — choose the city, date and time you are thinking in. This is usually your own.
  2. Add the other cities — the people you need on the call. Use a preset to add several at once.
  3. Check the working hours row — green means the slot is inside that city's day, red means somebody is being asked to join at a bad hour.
  4. Use the overlap strip — it scans all 24 hours of your reference day and marks the ones that fall inside everybody's working window.
  5. Send it — copy the text into a message, or download the .ics so each attendee's calendar does the conversion itself.

Why daylight saving breaks meeting plans

India does not observe daylight saving, so an IST clock never moves. Most of Europe and North America do, and they do not switch on the same dates — the United States moves in March and November, the European Union in late March and late October. For a few weeks each year the gap between Delhi and London or Delhi and New York is an hour different from what it was the week before.

That is why a recurring call set up in January starts arriving an hour early in April, and why a conversion you did once and wrote down stops being right. This planner asks the browser for the offset at the specific moment you chose, so the answer is correct for that date rather than for the date you happened to work it out on.

The southern hemisphere inverts it. Sydney is eleven hours ahead of UTC in January and ten in July, because its summer is the northern winter. A mental rule like "Australia is five and a half hours ahead of India" is right for part of the year and wrong for the rest.

Half-hour and quarter-hour zones

Not every zone is a whole number of hours from UTC. India is +5:30, Nepal is +5:45, parts of Australia are +9:30. If you plan by counting whole hours you will be thirty or forty-five minutes out for a large part of Asia. The times here are computed from the real offset, so these zones are handled the same as any other.

Common Use Cases

  • Client calls from India — find the window that is still afternoon in Delhi and already morning in New York, which is usually a narrow band.
  • Remote work and freelancing — check whether a posted "9 to 5 EST" role is actually workable from your city before applying.
  • Interview scheduling — confirm what a recruiter's "3pm" means in your time before you confirm the slot.
  • Exam and application deadlines — a deadline stated in one zone can fall on the previous day in yours. The date column makes that visible.
  • Family calls abroad — find the hour that is not the middle of the night for either side.
  • Release and deployment windows — a low-traffic hour in one region is peak in another.

About This Tool

Everything runs in your browser using its built-in time zone database, which is the same data your operating system uses. No times are sent anywhere, there is no account, and the tool works offline once the page has loaded.

Your chosen cities and working hours are remembered on your own device, so the planner opens with your usual set next time. Clearing your browser data clears them.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Does this handle daylight saving automatically?

Yes. The offset is read for the exact date and time you chose, not for today, so a meeting planned for a date after a clock change shows the correct time for that date.

Why does the date differ between cities?

Because a single moment can fall on different calendar days either side of the globe. When a city is on a different date the row shows it, which is the detail most often missed when a deadline is stated in another zone.

Why is the suggested slot sometimes empty?

Because no hour of that day falls inside everyone's working window — which is genuinely the case for some combinations, such as India with the US west coast. The tool says so rather than suggesting an hour that does not work. Widening your acceptable hours will usually produce a slot.

What does the .ics file contain?

One event at the exact moment you selected, stored in UTC, with the city list in the description. Each attendee's calendar converts it to their own zone, so nobody has to do the arithmetic.

Are half-hour zones like India and Nepal supported?

Yes. Offsets are taken from the browser's time zone data rather than assumed to be whole hours, so +5:30 and +5:45 are exact.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculation is local and the saved city list stays in your own browser storage.
