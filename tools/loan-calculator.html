About This Tool

The Loan Comparison Calculator is a comprehensive financial planning tool designed to help borrowers make informed decisions when choosing between multiple loan options. Whether you are considering a home loan, car loan, personal loan, or education loan, this calculator allows you to compare up to three loans simultaneously, giving you a clear picture of how each option impacts your finances over time.

Understanding the true cost of a loan goes beyond just looking at the interest rate. Factors like loan tenure, EMI amount, total interest paid, and prepayment options all play crucial roles in determining which loan is the best fit for your financial situation. This tool breaks down each of these components, presenting them in an easy-to-understand format with visual charts and detailed amortization schedules.

The amortization schedule shows you exactly how each monthly payment is split between principal and interest, helping you understand how your debt reduces over time. The prepayment calculator demonstrates potential savings when you make extra payments, motivating you to pay off loans faster. The eligibility estimator helps you understand your borrowing capacity based on your income and existing obligations, ensuring you don't over-leverage yourself. All calculations happen instantly in your browser with complete privacy — no data is ever sent to any server.

How is EMI calculated?

EMI is calculated using the standard formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1), where P is principal, r is monthly interest rate, and n is number of months. This is the same formula used by banks.

What is an amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule is a table showing how each monthly payment is divided between principal repayment and interest. In the early months, a larger portion goes toward interest, while later payments contribute more to principal reduction.

How does prepayment save money?

When you make extra payments, the additional amount goes directly toward reducing the principal. This reduces the interest charged in subsequent months, potentially saving you lakhs over the loan tenure and shortening the repayment period.

How is loan eligibility calculated?

Banks typically allow EMI obligations up to 50% of your monthly income. The eligibility estimator uses this ratio (minus existing EMIs) to calculate the maximum loan amount you can afford at the given interest rate and tenure.
