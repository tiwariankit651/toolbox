Frequently Asked Questions

Besides years, months and days, what else does it show?

After you pick a date of birth it fills a grid with total months, weeks, days, hours and minutes lived, the weekday you were born on, days until your next birthday and the weekday that birthday falls on. It also derives your Western zodiac, birthstone for your birth month, and Chinese zodiac animal from the birth year.

How does the Exam Eligibility Checker handle a cutoff date?

You enter a cutoff date plus a minimum and maximum age. It computes your completed years, months and days as on that cutoff, converts it to a decimal age (years + months/12 + days/365), and marks you eligible or not. It shows how far you are inside or past the upper limit, and notes informational relaxations (OBC +3, SC/ST +5) to verify against the official notification.

Does it remember dates I have already checked?

Yes. Your last date of birth is restored on your next visit, and each date you calculate is kept in a short history list (up to 10 and 12 entries depending on the list) stored only in your browser. Click any saved entry to reload it. You can also download or copy an age report of the current result.

How does the Retirement Countdown work?

Enter a retirement age (default 60) and it uses the date of birth above to build a retirement date — birth year plus the whole-number part of that age, same month and day. It then shows the date, the years/months/days remaining, and total days left. If that date has already passed, it tells you so instead of showing a countdown.

Why age arithmetic is harder than it looks

Subtracting two dates sounds trivial and has several genuine ambiguities that different systems resolve differently.

Leap years and the 29 February problem

Someone born on 29 February has no birthday in most years. For legal purposes different jurisdictions pick different answers — 28 February or 1 March — and a calculator has to choose one. It matters for age-limit eligibility by a single day.

Months have different lengths

From 31 January, adding one month could reasonably give 28 February, 2 March, or 3 March. There is no universally correct answer, which is why "age in months" can differ between tools by a day either side.

The convention most systems use, and the one worth assuming: the day of the month is preserved where it exists, and clamped to the last day of the month where it does not.

Completed years, not elapsed years

Age in most legal contexts is completed years. Someone whose birthday is next week is still the younger age today, even though they are a few days from the next. This is the rule that matters for exam eligibility and it is not the same as rounding.

Cut-off dates, which decide eligibility

Government recruitment states an age limit on a specific cut-off date, not on the date you apply or the date of the exam. Being within the limit today means nothing if the cut-off was six months ago.

This is the single most common eligibility error. The relevant calculation is your age on the stated cut-off date, and relaxations are applied to the limit rather than to your age.

Schools use the same mechanism for admission — age as on 31 March or 1 June of the relevant year — which is why a child born days apart can fall into different academic years.

Which date of birth counts

For Indian recruitment and examinations, the date on the matriculation certificate is treated as authoritative. If Aadhaar, a passport and the matriculation certificate disagree, the matriculation date is the one applications are checked against — and a mismatch surfaces at document verification, after you have cleared the exam.

Worth confirming the three agree before applying, because correcting a certificate takes considerably longer than an application window.

Time zones, briefly

A date of birth has no time zone attached, so age should be computed from calendar dates rather than from timestamps. A calculator that converts to UTC internally can be off by a day for someone born late in the evening — which is why age arithmetic should be done on the date components, not on milliseconds.
