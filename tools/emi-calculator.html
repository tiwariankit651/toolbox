The formula, so the output is not a black box

An EMI is the fixed monthly payment that clears a loan over a set term:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

where P is the principal, r the monthly rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the number of months. The payment stays constant while its composition changes: early instalments are mostly interest, later ones mostly principal.

On a ₹25 lakh loan at 8.5% over 20 years, the EMI is about ₹21,696. The first instalment contains roughly ₹17,708 of interest and ₹3,988 of principal. By the final year that has reversed almost entirely.

Why early repayment saves so much

This follows directly from the composition above and is the most useful thing to understand about a loan.

Interest each month is charged on the outstanding balance. Paying extra early reduces the balance for every remaining month, so the saving compounds. The same amount paid in year fifteen reduces the balance for only five years of interest.

A single extra payment in year two can shorten a twenty-year loan by several months. The same payment in year eighteen barely moves it.

Fixed, floating and reset dates

A floating rate loan changes with the benchmark, and lenders handle an increase in one of two ways: raise the EMI, or extend the tenure and keep the EMI the same. The second feels painless and costs considerably more in total interest.

Worth asking which your lender does, because an unchanged EMI after a rate rise means the tenure quietly grew.

Since October 2019, new floating-rate retail loans in India must be linked to an external benchmark such as the repo rate, which makes rate changes pass through faster in both directions.

What the EMI figure excludes

  • Processing fee, typically a percentage of the loan, paid upfront.
  • Insurance sometimes bundled and sometimes financed into the loan itself, which increases the principal.
  • Prepayment charges, which vary and are regulated differently for floating and fixed rate loans.
  • Documentation and valuation fees.

The useful comparison between two offers is not the EMI. It is the total outflow — EMI times tenure, plus every fee — because a lower EMI over a longer term usually costs more.

How much EMI is affordable

Lenders commonly work to a limit of around 40 to 50% of net monthly income for all EMIs combined, including existing ones. That is a lending constraint rather than a recommendation.

A more useful personal test: could you still pay this EMI after a month with no income, from savings? If not, the loan is sized to your best case rather than your actual one.

What is EMI?

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan. It includes both principal and interest components. The EMI amount depends on three factors: loan amount, interest rate, and loan tenure. Higher loan amount or interest rate means higher EMI, while longer tenure reduces EMI but increases total interest paid.
