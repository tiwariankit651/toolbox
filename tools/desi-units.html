About This Tool

Indian land measurement was never standardised. The acre and hectare are fixed worldwide, but the bigha, katha and biswa came from local revenue surveys that each used their own measuring rod — which is why one bigha is 27,225 square feet in Uttar Pradesh, 14,400 in West Bengal, 17,424 in Gujarat and only 6,806 in the Uttarakhand hills. Most online converters publish a single national figure, almost always the North Indian pucca bigha, and that figure is simply wrong in the majority of states. This converter starts from the state instead, shows the square footage behind each answer, and lists competing local conventions rather than silently choosing one. It also covers the stable units that paperwork actually uses — square yard or gaj, guntha, kanal, marla, cent, shatak, ground — plus traditional weights like quintal, maund, tola and seer, which still appear in mandi rates and jewellery pricing. Frequently Asked Questions

How many bigha are in one acre?

It depends on the state — there is no national answer. Roughly 1.6 bigha in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and pucca Rajasthan, 2 in Punjab and Haryana, 2.5 in Gujarat, 3.025 in West Bengal and Assam, 5 in Himachal Pradesh and about 6.4 in the Uttarakhand hills.

Why does the bigha vary so much?

Because it predates both the metric system and India's current state boundaries. Each regional revenue survey defined it from its own measuring rod, and those definitions survived into modern records. Unlike the acre, no authority ever standardised it.

What is the difference between pucca and kaccha bigha?

Pucca is the larger, officially recorded measure; kaccha is a smaller local one, often a half or a third of it. In Rajasthan the gap is 27,225 against 17,424 square feet, so ten bigha can mean two quite different plots.

Can I use these figures for property registration?

No. Treat them as planning estimates. Registration, mutation and mortgage paperwork must use the area recorded by your district land revenue office or measured by a licensed surveyor. Use this to understand a listing and to know what to verify.

Which units are actually fixed?

Square yard (gaj) is always 9 square feet, guntha is 1,089, kanal is 5,445, marla is 272.25, cent and shatak are 435.6, and killa is simply another name for the acre. These do not vary, which is why paperwork prefers them.

Is one quintal the same everywhere?

Yes. A quintal is 100 kg by definition and does not vary. The older maund did vary regionally; the figure here is the common 37.324 kg standard. Tola is 11.6638 grams, which is the standard used for gold.
