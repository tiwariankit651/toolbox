FAQ

Which format should I use for a retail product?

EAN-13 (most of the world, including India) or UPC-A (North America). These numbers must be licensed from GS1 — this tool only draws the bars.

What is a check digit?

The last digit of EAN/UPC/ITF-14 codes is computed from the others so a scanner can detect a misread. This tool computes it for you and flags a wrong one.

Why does my printed barcode not scan?

Usually too little quiet zone (white margin), low contrast, or bars printed too small. Keep dark bars on a light background and test before printing a run.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which barcode format should I use — retail product, shipping carton, or internal inventory?

For a retail product scanned at a checkout, use EAN-13 (used in India and most of the world) or UPC-A (North America). For a shipping or logistics carton, use ITF-14. For your own internal inventory, assets or IDs — where no public standard is required — use CODE128 (compact, handles letters and digits) or CODE39. The tool offers all of these plus EAN-8, UPC-E, ITF, MSI, Codabar and pharmacode.

What is the check digit, and can I leave it off?

EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A and ITF-14 end in a check digit that is calculated from the other digits using the GS1 modulo-10 formula, so a scanner can detect a misread. You can type the number with OR without it: enter 12 digits for EAN-13 and the tool appends the check digit and shows it; enter all 13 and it verifies the last one. If your check digit is wrong, the tool tells you the exact digit it expected (for example, for 590123412345 it expects 7, giving 5901234123457) instead of just saying 'invalid'.

I printed a barcode and it will not scan — why?

The three usual causes are the quiet zone, contrast and size. The quiet zone is the blank margin on each side; set the Margin option high enough (a common mistake is cropping it off). Contrast must be high — dark bars on a light background; the tool warns you if contrast is too low or if your bars are lighter than the background, because most scanners cannot read light-on-dark. Finally, bars printed too small or on a low-resolution printer blur together: export the PNG at 4× or 6× scale, or use the SVG for sharp printing at any size.

Can I generate a real retail barcode for selling a product?

This tool only draws the bars for whatever number you type — it does not assign you a number. Real EAN-13 and UPC-A numbers for products sold in shops must be licensed from GS1 (GS1 India for Indian sellers); they issue the company prefix that makes your product number globally unique. Use this tool to render a number you already own, to prototype labels, or for internal CODE128/CODE39 codes where no license is needed.

Picking a Symbology and Getting a Barcode That Actually Scans

Eleven symbologies, and when each one fits

This tool draws eleven 1D (linear) barcode types with JsBarcode, and the right choice depends on who reads the code. CODE128 is the workhorse for internal use: it encodes any ASCII text and digits and automatically switches between its A, B and C sub-modes to stay compact, which is why a mixed value like "FreeToolHubs-2026" fits cleanly. CODE39 is older and only covers uppercase A–Z, 0–9 and the symbols - . $ / + % and space — it is bulkier but still common in industry and defence. For retail products you want EAN-13 (the global and Indian standard) or UPC-A (North America), with EAN-8 and UPC-E as compact variants for small packs. Shipping cartons use ITF-14, built on interleaved 2 of 5; the plainer ITF, MSI and Codabar serve logistics, inventory and libraries; and pharmacode is a pharmaceutical packing control number.

How the tool handles the check digit

EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A and ITF-14 carry a trailing check digit computed from the earlier digits with the GS1 modulo-10 rule: weight the digits 3, 1, 3, 1 from the right, sum them, and the check digit is whatever brings the total to a multiple of ten. The tool implements this itself and also cross-checks the result against JsBarcode's own validator. That lets you type the number either way. Enter twelve digits for an EAN-13 and it appends and displays the thirteenth; enter all thirteen and it verifies the last one. If the check digit is wrong it does not just say "invalid" — it names the digit it expected, so 5901234123458 is reported as needing a 7, not an 8.

Validation and the character set

Each format checks its own allowed characters before rendering and gives a specific reason when something is off. Lowercase text sent to CODE39, for instance, produces a message telling you CODE39 is uppercase-only and showing your value converted to uppercase, rather than a silent failure. GS1 formats reject non-digits and wrong lengths with a message that states exactly how many digits they expect.
