📖 Cron Syntax Cheatsheet

A cron expression has five fields (plus an optional command):

┌───────────── minute        (0 - 59)
│ ┌─────────── hour          (0 - 23)
│ │ ┌───────── day of month  (1 - 31)
│ │ │ ┌─────── month         (1 - 12  or JAN-DEC)
│ │ │ │ ┌───── day of week    (0 - 6   or SUN-SAT, 0 = Sunday)
│ │ │ │ │
* * * * *  command to run

Allowed values

FieldRange
Minute0–59
Hour0–23
Day of month1–31
Month1–12 (JAN–DEC)
Day of week0–6 (SUN–SAT; 7 also = Sunday)

Special characters

  • * — every value (e.g. every minute). Example: * * * * *
  • , — list of values. Example: 0 9,17 * * * (9 AM and 5 PM)
  • - — range of values. Example: 0 9-17 * * * (every hour 9 AM–5 PM)
  • / — step values. Example: */15 * * * * (every 15 minutes)
  • L — "last" (last day of month/week). non-standard / Quartz
  • # — nth weekday of month, e.g. FRI#3. non-standard / Quartz
  • ? — "no specific value" (day-of-month vs day-of-week). non-standard / Quartz

Note: L, # and ? are supported by Quartz Scheduler and some cron implementations but are not part of standard Vixie/POSIX cron. This tool's builder and next-run preview use standard cron semantics.

Day-of-month & Day-of-week OR rule

When both day-of-month and day-of-week are restricted (neither is *), standard cron runs the job when either field matches — not both. This tool honors that OR behavior in the next-run preview.
