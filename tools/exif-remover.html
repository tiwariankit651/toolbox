About This Tool

When you take a photo, your phone embeds hidden data (EXIF metadata) including GPS location, camera model, date/time, and more. Sharing photos online with this data can expose your home address. This tool strips all metadata by re-drawing the image on a clean canvas. Supports multiple files at once. Your photos never leave your device.

What Gets Removed

  • GPS location coordinates
  • Camera make and model
  • Date and time taken
  • Lens information
  • All other EXIF, IPTC, and XMP data

Why EXIF Matters for Your Privacy

Every photo your phone or camera takes can embed hidden EXIF metadata — including the exact GPS coordinates where the shot was captured. When you post a geotagged photo online, anyone who downloads it can extract those coordinates and pinpoint your home, your child's school, or your daily routine. There are documented cases of stalkers and burglars using EXIF GPS data from social posts. It also reveals your camera/phone model, the date and time, and sometimes the software you used — a surprisingly detailed profile.

Which Platforms Strip EXIF (and Which Don't)

  • Strip EXIF automatically: Facebook, Instagram, Twitter/X, and WhatsApp remove most EXIF (including GPS) when you upload — but they may keep it server-side, and re-downloaded copies vary.
  • Often keep EXIF: Google Photos shared links, Flickr (by choice), personal websites, direct file sharing, email attachments, cloud storage links, and Discord all can preserve full EXIF including GPS.
  • Best practice: Never rely on a platform to protect you. Strip metadata yourself before sharing anywhere, especially when posting original files or sending images directly.
