🔄 Rotate & Flip Image

Rotate in 90° steps or by any free angle, flip horizontally or vertically, choose whether the canvas expands to fit or stays the original size. Full resolution is preserved. Everything runs in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

Drop one or more images — JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP and iPhone HEIC (batch supported)

How to Use This Tool

  1. Upload one photo, or several for batch processing. iPhone HEIC files are decoded in your browser.
  2. Rotate in clean 90° steps (left, right, 180°) or drag the free-angle slider to straighten a tilted horizon.
  3. Flip horizontally to mirror, or vertically.
  4. Pick how the canvas behaves — expand so nothing is clipped (corners filled with transparency or a colour you choose), or keep the original dimensions and crop the overhang.
  5. Download a single result, or process every uploaded file with the same settings and grab them as a ZIP.

Common Use Cases

  • Fixing a photo that imported sideways or upside-down
  • Straightening a crooked horizon or a document scanned at a slight angle
  • Mirroring a selfie so text reads the right way round
  • Rotating a whole folder of scanned pages the same way at once
  • Turning a landscape shot portrait for a story or print

About This Tool

Rotate and flip images without losing resolution. Ninety-degree steps and 180° are exact, lossless orientation changes. The free-angle slider rotates by any degree from −180 to +180; when you expand the canvas, the new size is computed from the rotated bounding box (width' = |w·cos θ| + |h·sin θ|, height' = |w·sin θ| + |h·cos θ|) so no pixel is ever clipped, and the triangular corners are filled with transparency or a colour you choose. Choosing "crop to original size" keeps the file's dimensions and lets the rotation overflow the edges, useful for a small straightening tweak. Flips are done with a canvas transform, not a filter, so they are exact. Drop several files to apply the same operation to all of them and download a ZIP built in your browser. The canvas re-encode also strips EXIF and GPS metadata.

Frequently Asked Questions

When I rotate by a free angle, why does the image get bigger?

With 'Expand to fit' selected, the canvas grows so the tilted image is never clipped. The new size comes from the rotated bounding box: width' = |w·cos θ| + |h·sin θ| and height' = |w·sin θ| + |h·cos θ|. For example a 4000×3000 photo rotated 30° becomes about 4964×4598. The triangular corners left over are filled with transparency or a colour you pick. Choose 'Crop to original size' instead if you only want a small straightening tweak and are happy to lose the overflowing edges.

Do the 90° and flip buttons reduce quality?

No. Rotating by 90°, 180° or 270° and flipping horizontally or vertically are done with an exact canvas transform at full resolution, so every pixel is preserved — these are lossless orientation changes. A 90° turn simply swaps the width and height. Only the free-angle slider resamples pixels, because a non-right-angle rotation has to interpolate; it still works at full resolution with high-quality smoothing.

Can I rotate a whole folder of images the same way?

Yes. Drop several files and an 'Apply to all & download ZIP' button appears. It runs your exact current operation — the 90° steps, flips, free angle, canvas mode and background — on every image and packs the results into a ZIP built in your browser with JSZip. Files that can't be decoded are skipped and reported rather than failing the whole batch.

What happens to corners, format and metadata?

For free angles the exposed corners are transparent (keep PNG or WebP to preserve that) or filled with a colour of your choice (needed for JPG, which has no transparency). You can keep the original format or export JPG, PNG or WebP with a quality slider. The canvas re-encode drops EXIF and GPS metadata, and because phone photos are decoded upright first, a rotation is applied on top of the already-correct orientation.

Right-angle turns are free; free angles cost pixels

There are two completely different things happening behind the rotate buttons, and knowing which one you are using explains the result. Turning an image 90°, 180° or 270°, or flipping it, only rearranges pixels that already exist — no pixel is invented or destroyed, so the quality is identical to the original. Rotating by an arbitrary angle, say 7° to straighten a horizon, is different: the output grid no longer lines up with the input grid, so every pixel has to be interpolated from its neighbours. That is unavoidable for any tool, and this one at least does it at full resolution with high-quality smoothing rather than on a shrunken preview.

The bounding-box maths behind "expand to fit"

When you rotate by a free angle and ask the canvas to expand, the image has to grow or its tilted corners would be clipped. The new dimensions come straight from trigonometry of the rotated rectangle: width' = |w·cos θ| + |h·sin θ| and height' = |w·sin θ| + |h·cos θ|. For a 4000×3000 photo at 30° that works out to roughly 4964×4598. You can verify it yourself — the output size is printed under the preview. The leftover triangular corners are transparent by default, which PNG and WebP preserve; export to JPG and you must choose a fill colour because JPG has no transparency.

Expand versus crop to original size

The canvas mode changes what you keep. "Expand to fit" guarantees nothing is lost, at the price of a larger file with filled corners — right for turning a landscape shot fully portrait, or any rotation you want intact. "Crop to original size" keeps the file's original dimensions and lets the rotation spill over the edges, so the corners of the picture are trimmed away. That is what you want for a small straightening nudge of a degree or two, where expanding would add ugly wedges for no benefit. A pure 90° turn in crop mode simply swaps width and height, since that is the honest "original size" after a quarter turn.

Flipping, and why it isn't the same as rotating

Flipping mirrors the image across an axis; rotating turns it. A horizontal flip is what you need when a selfie came out with text backwards, or to face a portrait the other way. It is done with a canvas scale of −1 on one axis, which is exact and reversible — flip twice and you are back where you started. Horizontal and vertical flips combine freely with any rotation, and the preview shows every transform applied together so there are no surprises at download.

Batch, format and metadata

Drop several files and the same operation — steps, flips, free angle, canvas mode, fill colour — applies to all of them, packed into a ZIP made in your browser. Unreadable files are skipped and reported rather than aborting the run. Output can keep the original format or convert to JPG, PNG or WebP, and because every image is rebuilt on a canvas, EXIF and GPS metadata are dropped from the saved files. Phone photos are decoded upright first, so your rotation is applied on top of the correct orientation, never fighting it.
