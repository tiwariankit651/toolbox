How to Read an Official Notification Properly

The single most important document in any competitive exam journey is the official notification, sometimes called the advertisement or information bulletin. Everything on this page is a convenience summary — the notification is the legally binding source of truth. When it is released, download the full PDF and read it end to end at least once before you fill anything. Aspirants routinely skim the first page, note the dates, and miss a clause that quietly disqualifies them weeks later.

Read the eligibility section slowly. Check the exact cut-off date on which age and qualification are measured — it is often the first day of a month, not the day you apply, and a difference of a few days can decide eligibility. Confirm whether a final-year student can apply provisionally, whether a specific percentage is mandatory, and whether your degree is on the recognised list. Then read the number of attempts permitted, category-wise age relaxation, and any domicile or language requirement for state exams. Note the fee, the payment window, and whether the fee is refundable on appearing, as several railway and banking exams refund most of it. Finally, locate the syllabus, exam pattern, negative-marking rule, and the schedule for admit cards and results. Keep the notification file saved locally; portals sometimes replace it with a corrigendum, and you want the version you applied under.

Documents to Prepare in Advance

Nothing derails an application faster than scrambling for documents on the last day. Prepare a personal document folder well before any notification drops, because portals have strict and unforgiving specifications. A passport-size photograph is usually required in JPEG format, roughly 3.5 cm by 4.5 cm, between 20 KB and 50 KB, on a plain white or light background, taken recently. Signatures are typically needed on plain white paper in black or blue ink, scanned or photographed, then reduced to a small file, often around 140 by 60 pixels and under 50 KB. Some forms demand a thumb impression and a handwritten declaration photographed to a size limit as well.

To hit these specs without losing quality, crop first, then resize to the exact pixel dimensions, and compress last. Our image resizer and image compressor handle those steps in the browser, and the signature maker helps you produce a clean signature image. A few state and board forms ask for a date to be stamped on the photograph; the date on photo tool adds it in an accepted format. Beyond images, keep scanned copies of your Class 10 and 12 marksheets, degree certificate or provisional certificate, caste or category certificate with a recent issue date where reservation is claimed, EWS certificate if applicable, domicile proof for state exams, a PwBD certificate if relevant, and a government photo ID such as Aadhaar. Verify that your name is spelled identically across all documents; a mismatch between your marksheet and your ID is a common reason for rejection at the verification stage.

Common Application Mistakes That Cause Rejection

Rejections rarely happen because a candidate was under-qualified; far more often they happen because of avoidable clerical errors. The most frequent are uploading a photograph or signature in the wrong format or size, using an old or unclear photo, and swapping the photo and signature fields. Others include entering a name, date of birth, or category that does not match the supporting certificate, selecting the wrong exam centre and being unable to change it, and not completing the payment so the application stays in draft. On mobile browsers, form fields sometimes do not scroll fully, leaving mandatory boxes blank without the candidate noticing.

  • Fill the form on a laptop or desktop where possible, and if you must use a phone, request the desktop site.
  • Double-check spelling of your name, parents' names, date of birth and category against your certificates before final submission.
  • Confirm the photo and signature are in the correct fields, format and size, and are clearly visible in the preview.
  • Complete the payment and wait for the confirmation; a pending transaction is not a submitted application.
  • After submitting, save the PDF acknowledgement, note the application or registration number, and take a screenshot of the confirmation screen. If you need to combine your documents, the PDF merger helps.

Planning a Study Timeline Backwards From the Exam Date

The most reliable way to build a preparation plan is to work backwards from the tentative exam date rather than forwards from today. Start by writing down the exam date, then reserve the final three to four weeks purely for revision and full-length mock tests — do not plan to learn new topics in that window. Next, block time for a first complete pass through the syllabus, and leave a buffer of a couple of weeks for slippage, because something always takes longer than expected. Whatever remains is your real learning time, and dividing the syllabus across it gives you honest weekly targets instead of vague intentions.

Translate those weekly targets into a concrete schedule and track them. The timetable maker turns your weekly hours into a colour-coded grid, the syllabus tracker shows what percentage of the syllabus is genuinely covered, and the exam calendar keeps notification, admit-card and result dates in view. For subjects heavy on formulas, a formula sheet keeps quick references at hand, and the quiz maker lets you test recall instead of passively re-reading. Reviewing a topic a day after learning it, again after a few days, and once more after a week embeds it far better than a single long session, so schedule those revisits deliberately rather than hoping to find time for them.

How to Verify Dates on Official Sites

Because the dates on this page are tentative, treat them only as a planning signal and verify before you act. Go directly to the conducting body's official website — every exam card here links to it — and type the address yourself or use a bookmark rather than following unofficial links from search results or social media, which are frequently outdated or misleading. Look for the notification, notice board, or what's-new section, and check the publication date on the notice so you know it is current. Cross-check the exam date, application window and admit-card date against the notification PDF rather than a summary, since portals sometimes update one and not the other. Be alert to corrigenda, which are official amendments that can change dates or eligibility after the first notification. When in doubt, the official website and its helpdesk are the only authorities that matter; this hub is a starting point, not a substitute.
