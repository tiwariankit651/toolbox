Shipping a Favicon That Survives Small Sizes and Dark Tabs

Why the output is PNG, not .ico

A classic favicon is a multi-resolution .ico, but that container cannot be assembled reliably from a browser canvas, so this tool generates PNGs instead. That is not a shortcut with consequences — every current browser accepts a PNG icon through <link rel="icon" type="image/png">, and the HTML snippet it produces already declares those links. The only scenario where you still need an .ico is a very old browser, in which case you take the 32×32 PNG to a dedicated ICO converter. For everything modern, the PNG set is complete.

The three modes and what the canvas actually does

Text/Initials draws one or two characters on a solid or gradient background, with font, colour, shape and padding controls; padding works by shrinking the drawable box and scaling the text to fit, so a high padding value makes the glyph smaller rather than cropping it. Emoji mode renders the chosen emoji as text at 75% of the icon size, optionally on a background. Image Upload is the most involved: it takes a square crop driven by zoom and horizontal/vertical position sliders.

  • "Fill transparent areas" paints your chosen colour behind a transparent PNG so the icon is not see-through on a coloured tab bar.
  • "Trim transparent padding" scans the image's alpha channel to find the non-transparent bounding box and crops to it, which fixes logos that have large empty margins and appear tiny at 16px.
  • Shape (square, rounded, circle) is a clipping mask applied at every size.

Checking legibility before you ship

The single most useful habit this tool encourages is the Live Preview. It renders your icon inside a browser tab, a bookmark bar, an iOS home-screen tile and an Android round launcher, so you see how aggressively the 16px version loses detail. Ticking "Dark-mode preview" repaints those mockups with dark backgrounds, which is how you catch the common failure of a dark-coloured icon vanishing against a dark tab bar. Decide your colours against both backgrounds, not just the editor's.

Remember that two-letter initials and fine logo detail rarely read at 16×16 — the small canvases next to the main preview exist precisely so you can confirm the smallest size still communicates.

The package contents and how to install them

Download Full Package produces a ZIP of PNGs at 16, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, 150, 180, 192 and 512 pixels. Two are named for their purpose: 180 becomes apple-touch-icon.png for iOS, and 150 becomes mstile-150x150.png for Windows tiles. The ZIP also includes a ready-to-paste favicon-links.html snippet and a site.webmanifest that declares the 192 and 512 icons plus a 512 "any maskable" entry for installable web apps.

To install, copy the link tags into your page's <head> and place the PNGs at the paths they reference (the snippet uses root-relative paths like /favicon-32x32.png, so adjust them if your icons live in a subfolder). Everything is generated in your browser; your colour, font and shape choices are remembered locally between visits, so repeated projects start from your last settings.
