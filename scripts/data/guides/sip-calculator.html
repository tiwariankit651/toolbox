Interpreting SIP Projections: Compounding, Inflation and the Crossover Year

How each mode compounds, month by month

The calculator does not use a single closed-form formula for everything — it simulates each mode month by month, which keeps the five modes consistent. For a plain SIP it adds your monthly amount and applies one-twelfth of the annual rate every month. Step-up SIP does the same but raises the monthly contribution by your step-up percentage at the start of each year, so later years invest more. Lumpsum compounds a single opening amount. SWP starts from a corpus, grows it monthly, then subtracts your withdrawal, flooring the balance at zero once the money runs out. Goal Planner inverts the SIP maths to find the monthly amount needed to hit a target.

Because compounding is monthly, the maturity figure will differ slightly from a yearly-compounding estimate — this is the more realistic model for a monthly SIP.

Why the real value matters more than the headline number

The three cards show invested, returns and total value, but the figure worth internalising is the inflation-adjusted value printed below them. It divides the nominal corpus by (1 + inflation rate) raised to the number of years, expressing the result in today's purchasing power. A large maturity value at 6% inflation over a long horizon can shrink substantially in real terms, which is the point of surfacing it. Amounts are formatted in the Indian lakh/crore system and also spelled out in words to prevent misreading a figure by a digit.

Reading the year table, chart and the crossover

The year-by-year table breaks each year into opening balance, amount invested, growth and closing value, so you can watch the growth column overtake the invested column. The chart plots cumulative invested against total value as two lines; the gap between them is your return. The exported PDF makes this explicit by stating the first year in which the gain exceeds the amount you have put in — the "crossover" where compounding starts doing more work than your contributions. That single line is often more instructive than the final total.

What the numbers are not

Every figure here is a projection at a constant assumed rate, not a forecast. Real mutual funds return different amounts each year and carry market risk, so a steady 12% line is a planning convenience, not a promise — the tool states this and is explicitly not financial advice, recommending a SEBI-registered advisor for actual decisions. A sound way to use it is to run a pessimistic, a moderate and an optimistic rate through the compare feature rather than trusting one number. Your inputs are saved locally in your browser so you can return and adjust assumptions; the calculation itself never leaves your device.
