How the Tracker Groups Spending, Dates Transactions and Protects Your Data

Everything is scoped to the month you pick

The month selector at the top is not a filter you can ignore — it drives the entire interface. The dashboard totals, the transaction list, the budget bars and the insights all read only the transactions whose date falls inside the selected month. A transaction belongs to a month purely by the year-month portion of its date, so if an entry seems to vanish, check that you are viewing the month its date lands in rather than assuming it was lost.

This also explains the "Last 6 Months" bar chart: it walks back six months from your selected month and sums income and expenses for each, which is why switching months shifts the whole six-month window rather than redrawing a fixed calendar range.

The 50/30/20 split and where custom categories land

The Insights tab maps each built-in category to needs, wants or savings and reports the percentage split as guidance, not advice. The mapping is fixed in the tool: rent, groceries, utilities, mobile, transport, education, medical, EMI, insurance and family support are needs; dining, shopping, entertainment and subscriptions are wants; the savings/investment category is savings. Two behaviours worth knowing:

  • Any custom category you create is treated as a "want" by default, so a custom "needs" expense will skew the split unless you account for it mentally.
  • Leftover income — income minus total expenses, when positive — is added to the savings bucket, so a high savings percentage can reflect simply not spending rather than money moved to investments.

Dates, recurring items and the local-time fix

A plain transaction carries a date, category, payment method and note. A recurring item (rent, an EMI, a subscription) is stored separately as just a description, amount and category; the "+ this month" button turns it into a real transaction dated to the first of the selected month and tagged "(recurring)". Recurring items are templates — adding one twice creates two transactions.

Dates are computed from your local calendar day, not UTC. This matters in India: a UTC-based date would roll over at 5:30 AM local time, so an entry added just after midnight could be filed under the previous day and break a running total. The tool uses local year-month-day throughout to avoid that off-by-one.

Budget colours, backups and what import replaces

Budget bars are a per-category, per-month limit. The bar turns amber once spending reaches 80% of the limit and red at 100% and beyond, and the width caps at 100% even when you overspend — so read the percentage label, not just the bar, to see how far over you are.

All data lives in your browser's local storage with no account. To move or archive it, Export JSON gives a re-importable backup, while the Accountant ZIP additionally bundles a transactions CSV, a plain-text monthly summary with per-category totals, and a README. Importing a JSON backup replaces your current data entirely after a confirmation prompt, and the importer accepts only JSON files this tool produced — a CSV or arbitrary file is rejected rather than silently mangling your records.
