Matching RSVP speed to what you are actually reading

This tool flashes words one chunk at a time (RSVP, rapid serial visual presentation) so your eyes stay fixed and skip the saccades that slow normal reading. The catch is that comprehension drops if the speed outruns your processing, so the right setting depends on the material. The presets exist precisely because three separate controls — speed, chunk size, and font — need to move together, and guessing them individually rarely lands on a usable combination.

What each preset changes, and the tradeoff

Each preset writes all three controls at once: Beginner is 180 WPM, 1 word, 52px; Normal is 250 WPM, 1 word, 42px; Fast is 400 WPM with 2-word chunks; Skim is 650 WPM with 3-word chunks; Sprint is 900 WPM with 5-word chunks. Notice the pattern: higher speeds also widen the chunk and shrink the font. Larger chunks are how the tool sustains high WPM without each individual word becoming an impossible flash, but wide chunks defeat the single-focus-point benefit of RSVP. For dense study material, a narrower chunk at a moderate WPM usually retains more than Sprint.

How the pacing and punctuation pause work

The base delay per chunk is 60000 / WPM × (words per chunk) milliseconds, so WPM is counted across words, not flashes. With "Pause on punctuation" on, the tool inspects the last character of the chunk: a period, exclamation mark, or question mark doubles that chunk's delay, while a comma, semicolon, or colon multiplies it by 1.3. This reintroduces the natural micro-rests of sentence and clause boundaries. "Ramp up speed gradually" only affects the first 20 chunks, scaling WPM from half speed up to full, which is useful for easing in rather than being hit with full pace cold.

Reading the WPM feedback honestly

The panel shows two different numbers. Your set WPM drives the ETA. The "actual WPM" is measured from real elapsed time since you pressed Play, so it includes every pause you took. If actual WPM is far below your target, you were pausing or the punctuation multiplier is slowing you — that is expected, not a bug. Comprehension scores from the built-in quiz passages ("The Indian Economy", "Space Exploration") attach to your most recent session, letting you see whether a higher speed cost you accuracy.

Importing text and splitting long documents

  • Imports accept .txt and .md up to 5MB. The tool rejects files containing null bytes or more than three Unicode replacement characters, which catches binaries renamed to .txt, and refuses empty files.
  • "Split into session ZIP" divides your text into ~10-minute sittings using WPM × 10 words per part, names them part-01.txt and up, and adds a README. If the whole text fits in one session it tells you there is nothing to split.
  • History, settings, and your chosen preset persist locally between visits, so your reading setup is restored automatically.
