Rearranging a PDF page by page without re-rendering it

Organize PDF treats a document as a tray of cards you can shuffle, turn, drop or duplicate. It is aimed at the everyday mess of scanned files: pages in the wrong order, a couple turned sideways, a stray blank in the middle, or a cover that belongs in front of everything. Because the work is done with pdf-lib and pdf.js in the browser, the file is never uploaded, and the pages that come out are the original pages — not flattened images.

Dragging that works with a mouse and a finger

Reordering uses pointer events rather than the old HTML5 drag-and-drop API. That choice matters: HTML5 dragging simply does not fire on most touch screens, so a mobile user would be stuck. Here the same press-and-move gesture drives reordering on a laptop and a phone alike, and as you move over a card a marker shows whether the dragged page will land before or after it. For keyboard users, or when precise dragging is fiddly, every card also carries ▲ and ▼ buttons that nudge it one position at a time. A small badge on each card shows its current place in the sequence so you always know what position you are editing.

Deleting safely, and acting on many pages at once

Tap a card to highlight it; tap again to clear it. With one or more pages selected you can rotate or delete them together, which is far quicker than working card by card. Deletion is reversible: the removed pages are held, and an "Undo delete" button — labelled with how many pages are waiting — puts them back in their original slots. Undo covers the most recent deletion, so delete a batch, check the result, and restore it in one click if it was wrong. Whole-document controls round this out: reverse the entire order, sort everything back to the original order, select all, or clear the selection.

Adding, duplicating and inserting pages

Three actions grow the document rather than just rearrange it. The duplicate button on a card drops an identical copy directly after it, handy for repeating a form page. "Blank page at end" inserts an empty page sized to match the first page in the grid, useful as a separator or a signature sheet. "Add pages from another PDF" appends every page of a second file, which then behave like any other card — drag them wherever they belong. A second file that is password-protected or unreadable is skipped with a message, so one bad file never derails the batch. Thumbnails for large documents are rendered in small batches so a long PDF does not freeze the tab while previews catch up.

What the saved file contains

Saving copies each page with pdf-lib's copyPages, which preserves the original content exactly. Any rotation you applied in the grid is added on top of the page's existing rotation, so a page already stored rotated ends up correct rather than double-counted. Blank pages are written at their chosen size. The output downloads as your-file-organized.pdf, and the page count shown above the grid is your quick check that the result matches what you arranged.
