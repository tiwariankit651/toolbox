Why the two regimes can disagree so sharply on the same income

This calculator computes tax under both the Old and New regimes for FY 2025-26 and FY 2026-27 (Budget 2026 left the slab rates and the Section 87A rebate unchanged, so the figures are identical across those years) and tells you which costs less. The gap between the two often surprises people, and it comes down to a single structural difference: the New regime trades away almost every deduction in exchange for lower, wider slabs, while the Old regime keeps higher rates but lets you shrink taxable income through investments and allowances.

The slabs and standard deductions the code actually uses

The New regime subtracts a ₹75,000 standard deduction, then taxes in bands: nil up to ₹4L, 5% to ₹8L, 10% to ₹12L, 15% to ₹16L, 20% to ₹20L, 25% to ₹24L, and 30% above that. The Old regime subtracts a ₹50,000 standard deduction and uses a 0/5/20/30% structure, where the first exempt band depends on age — ₹2.5L below 60, ₹3L for 60-80, and ₹5L above 80. Both then add a 4% health and education cess, and surcharge applies only at the higher income thresholds (10% above ₹50L, rising to 37% above ₹5 crore).

The rebate and marginal relief that flatten tax near ₹12L

Under the New regime, if taxable income is ₹12L or below the tool applies a Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000, which typically zeroes the tax. Just above ₹12L it applies marginal relief so the tax cannot exceed the amount by which income crosses ₹12L. This is why a ₹13L gross (₹12.25L taxable) shows roughly ₹26,000 (₹25,000 plus cess) rather than a full ₹66,300 — the relief caps the jump. The Old regime's rebate is smaller: up to ₹12,500, and only when taxable income is ₹5L or below.

Deduction caps and the HRA helper

Only the Old regime uses your deductions, and the code enforces statutory ceilings regardless of what you type: 80C is capped at ₹1.5L, 80CCD(1B) NPS at ₹50,000, and home-loan interest under 24(b) at ₹2L. The HRA helper takes the least of three figures — actual HRA received, 50% of basic (metro) or 40% (non-metro), and rent paid minus 10% of basic — and writes the result into the HRA field. A practical check: if the New regime wins for you, adding more deductions will not change that unless they push the Old regime below it.

What the result does not cover

  • The exports (PDF via pdf-lib, and CSV) list your income, age group, each claimed deduction with a note when it hit its cap, and both regimes' tax, effective rate, and in-hand figures. The PDF prints "Rs." because the standard font cannot encode the rupee glyph.
  • Capital gains and business income are not modelled — it assumes salary plus other income only.
  • The figures are planning estimates. The page itself flags that you should confirm liability with a qualified professional before filing, and slabs can change in a future Budget.
